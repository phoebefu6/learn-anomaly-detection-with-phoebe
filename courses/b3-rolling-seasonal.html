From global to contextual

In b2 you built the statistical detectors - z-score, IQR, and robust MAD - and felt their limit: they judge every point against the whole dataset, so they miss anything that is only odd for its moment. Tonight you fix that. Rolling and seasonal detectors compare each point to what was normal right there - its recent neighbours, or its weekday - and that is what finally catches the contextual anomaly. Part 1 · local context

Judge each point against its neighbours 6 min live

A rolling detector keeps a moving window of recent days and asks: is today far from the local mean, in units of the local standard deviation? Because the window travels with the series, it adapts to a drifting trend that would fool a global threshold. EWMA does the same with an exponentially-weighted memory instead of a hard window.

LiveRolling and EWMA, and where they still fail3 min▶
  • Rolling z-score - local mean and std over a trailing window; flags points beyond k local sigmas. Adapts to trend and slow drift.
  • EWMA - an exponentially-weighted moving average; recent points count more, no hard window edge. Smooth and responsive.
  • The catch - if the window spans the weekly cycle, the seasonal swing inflates the local std and the weekend dips look like anomalies. Rolling fixes trend, not seasonality. That is what the seasonal detector is for.
Real world

Rolling detectors are the backbone of ops monitoring. Most metric-alerting systems are, under the hood, a rolling mean and a band. They are cheap, streaming-friendly, and explainable - which is why they are everywhere. Their weakness is exactly the seasonal one you are about to fix.

Part 2 · seasonal context

Subtract the pattern, then flag the remainder 5 min live

The strongest simple detector on seasonal data does one clever thing: it removes the expected weekly pattern first. Decompose the series into trend, season, and residual (STL), and run your detector on the residual alone. Now a weekday-at-weekend-level jumps out, because against its own weekday it is far from normal.

Both are a few lines on the Lumen series from b1:

★ Run it now (Python) - rolling + STL-residualimport numpy as np, pandas as pd from statsmodels.tsa.seasonal import STL # 1) rolling z-score (local context, window = 10) mu = s.rolling(10, min_periods=4).mean() sd = s.rolling(10, min_periods=4).std() roll_z = (s - mu) / sd roll_flags = set(np.where(np.abs(roll_z) > 2.5)[0]) # 2) STL-residual detector (seasonal context) res = STL(s, period=7, robust=True).fit().resid med = res.median(); mad = (res - med).abs().median() or 1 mz = 0.6745 * (res - med) / mad # robust z on the residual stl_flags = set(np.where(np.abs(mz) > 3)[0]) for name, f in [("rolling", roll_flags), ("STL-residual", stl_flags)]: tp = len(f & set(truth)); fp = len(f - set(truth)); fn = len(set(truth) - f) p = tp/(tp+fp) if tp+fp else 0; r = tp/(tp+fn) if tp+fn else 0 print(f"{name:13s} P {p:.2f} R {r:.2f} (caught {tp}/{len(truth)})")

The STL-residual detector is the one that finally catches the contextual anomaly - because on its own weekday, it is far from normal.

Sensitivity is a business decision Turn the sensitivity up and recall rises but false alarms flood in; turn it down and precision rises but you miss real events. There is no free lunch - where you set it depends on the cost of a missed anomaly versus the cost of a false alarm. That is a conversation with the business, not just a hyperparameter.

Real world

Deseasonalizing is the highest-leverage move in time-series detection. Before reaching for isolation forests or autoencoders, remove the known pattern - weekday, holiday, campaign - and detect on what is left. Half of "our model is too noisy" turns out to be a seasonal pattern nobody subtracted.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What does a rolling-window detector fix that a global z-score does not?

The window travels with the series, so it adapts to a drifting trend. It does NOT fix seasonality - a weekly swing inside the window still trips it.

2 · Why does the STL-residual detector catch the contextual anomaly?

Subtracting the seasonal component leaves a flat residual. Against its own weekday, the contextual point now stands out - which it never did globally.

3 · Turning sensitivity up does what?

Sensitivity is a precision/recall trade-off. More sensitive = catch more real anomalies but flag more false ones. Where you set it is a business decision.
